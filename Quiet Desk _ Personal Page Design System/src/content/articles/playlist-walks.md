there's a specific walk i take. from the library to my dorm, about twenty-five minutes if i don't take shortcuts. longer if it's cold and i'm procrastinating. most of the time i put something on and don't really listen to it.

this playlist is different. i made it for the version of this walk where i actually want to be there — where it's not in between two other things, but the thing itself.

it took a while to get right.

## how i think about playlists

a good playlist isn't a list of songs you like. it's a sequence. it has a shape. the way a conversation has a shape — there's an entry point, a middle where the stakes feel real, and something like a resolution, or at least a good place to stop.

most of mine are too comfortable. everything at the same energy, the same tempo. good for background noise, bad for actually feeling something. this one i built differently. i let the hard songs in.

## the first section (tracks 1–7)

the first seven tracks are what i think of as "still inside." it's the feeling of leaving somewhere — the library, a lecture, a long conversation — and not quite being outside yet. your body is walking but your head is still in whatever you just left.

these are mostly instrumental. a few have vocals but they're buried. Nils Frahm's *Says* is in here somewhere. it doesn't ask anything of you.

## the middle (tracks 8–18)

this is the part i'm most proud of. it's where the walk becomes actual walking — you've shaken off the thing you just left, you're not home yet, you're just moving through a city at night.

i put Phoebe Bridgers in here. *Savior Complex*, which sounds like a terrible idea for a walk playlist, but something about it feels exactly right at the twenty-minute mark when you're crossing the bridge and there's nobody around. it's the kind of song that makes me feel less strange about being a person with feelings who also has a compilers assignment due.

also: *Motion Picture Soundtrack* by Radiohead. this is me refusing to apologize.

## the last six

the last six tracks are slow. deliberately slow. slower than walking. i've never understood playlists that end on a high — you get home and then what, you have to come down from it? the walk should land. you should arrive.

there's a Sufjan Stevens song at the very end. i won't tell you which one because if you know, you know, and if you don't, i don't want to ruin the moment.

## what i actually listen to

i should be honest: i often get three songs in and then my brain goes somewhere else and i stop paying attention. the playlist runs and i don't notice. i've listened to the full thing maybe four times.

but those four times were good. that's enough. that's what it's for.
